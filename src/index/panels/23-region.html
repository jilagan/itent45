Where you run it is a decision, and physics gets a vote

Panel 03 showed that most of a page load is round trips, and that no amount of server capacity fixes a slow one. This is the panel that answers it. A region is a geographic place with datacentres in it; an availability zone is one of those datacentres, far enough from its neighbours to fail separately and close enough to replicate to instantly. Choosing between them is a trade between speed, survival and money, and you cannot have all three.

The physics figure is the hard floor, two-way distance divided by the speed of light in glass. Real networks land at roughly 1.5× that, because packets go through switches and rarely travel in a straight line.

Nothing you write, buy or optimize gets you under the floor. Only moving closer does.

What this means for your capstone Every one of your users is in Metro Manila, so put the servers in Singapore and stop thinking about it. Take multi-AZ if the provider gives it to you by default, because it costs almost nothing and removes an entire category of disaster. Do not go multi-region: you would be paying double, writing conflict-resolution code, and accepting a data-loss window, in order to survive an event that has never happened to your users. Multi-region is what you reach for when a region going down costs more than running two of everything, and you should be able to say what that number is before you do it.
