Entities, attributes and instances

A data model starts as a pile of nouns. Each noun is one of three kinds.

Think of a class list in a spreadsheet. The sheet is about students. Each column holds one fact, such as the ID number, and each row is one student. A data model gives these three parts their own names.

Think first. A class meets in SOM 407. Is Room an attribute of the class or an entity of its own? Write down your choice and one reason.
Collect a few answers before moving on. The question comes back in the final step, and the deciding test is in "More detail" of the next step: does the room have facts of its own, and is it shared by many classes?

Choose entity, attribute or instance for each term. An explanation appears after each answer.

How to tell them apart

Entity: a person, place, object, event or concept the organization keeps data about. It has many examples, and each one can be told apart from the others.

Attribute: a property of an entity. It describes nothing on its own and only makes sense when it is attached to an entity.

Instance: one example of an entity, stored as a row of data. You store instances, and you do not draw a box for each one.

Room: attribute or entity?

If a room is only a name such as SOM 407, it is an attribute of the class. If the school also records its capacity or whether it has a projector, or lists everything scheduled in one room, Room is an entity of its own.

Student and Course are easy to spot. Enrollment is easy to miss, because it feels like something that happened. It has a date, a status and a grade, and none of those belong to the student or the course alone. So the pairing is an entity.

You can now sort things into entities, attributes and instances.
