02 — Encapsulation

Your message travels inside a stack of envelopes

A protocol is just an agreement about the shape of a message. Each layer takes what it was handed, wraps it in its own header, and passes it down. The receiving machine unwraps in reverse. Nobody talks across layers. Each layer only talks to its counterpart on the far end.

Ask the room Click through the layers. Notice that the thing you wrote (GET /courses) never changes. Everything else is packaging. So: how many bytes of packaging does it cost to send 24 bytes of message?

What happens when it does not fit in one envelope

A network link will not carry a message of any size. Ethernet tops out at about 1,500 bytes per frame, so anything larger is chopped into pieces and every piece gets its own full set of envelopes. Drag the response size and watch one reply become many.

How big is the reply?
Each packet carries 1,460 bytes of your data and 54 bytes of Ethernet, IP and TCP headers.

Press Send it and watch them leave. They do not all go at once: TCP starts by sending about ten and waiting to see if they arrive, then doubles. That waiting is why a large reply costs round trips, not just bandwidth.
