Relational or not is a question about your questions

Supabase gives you Postgres. Firebase gives you documents. Both will store your capstone. The choice is usually made on which tutorial you found first, so here is the version that is actually about your data: the same enrolments in both shapes, and the same three questions asked of each.

The two families, and what "schema" means in each

The split people mean by SQL and NoSQL is really a split about where the schema is enforced. Both have one. Only one of them makes the database responsible for it.

Structured · the database enforces it
Relational

Tables with fixed columns and declared types. The schema is written down once and the database refuses anything that does not fit. Joins let you ask questions you did not plan for.

Postgres · MySQL · SQL Server · Supabase
Unstructured · your code enforces it
Document

Records are self-describing documents. Two documents in the same collection can have different fields, so a typo becomes a new field rather than an error. The schema still exists, it just lives in your application.

MongoDB · Firebase · DynamoDB
Unstructured · barely a schema at all
Key-value

A key, a blob, and nothing else. Extremely fast and extremely dumb. Used as a cache in front of something else more often than as a system of record.

Redis · Memcached
Unstructured · a different question
Graph

Stores the relationships as first-class things rather than as foreign keys. Worth knowing exists. Reach for it when the question is "how is this connected to that", several hops deep.

Neo4j · Neptune
"Unstructured" does not mean you get to skip the modelling The entities, attributes and relationships from panels 09 and 10 are still there. A document store does not remove them, it moves the job of enforcing them from the database into your code, where nobody can see it and every client has to remember it. That is sometimes a good trade. It is never a way of avoiding the design.
What each shape is actually good at
Rows are good at questions you did not plan for

Any column can be filtered, joined or grouped after the fact. You do not have to know the questions when you design the schema, which matters because you do not.

→ the cost is a join, priced in panel 15
Documents are good at one question, answered fast

The whole page in one read, no join, no assembly. Brilliant when the access pattern is fixed and known. Painful the first time somebody asks a question sideways to it.

→ you denormalized, so panel 14 applies
Constraints live in one of them

Two students cannot take the last seat if the database refuses, which is panel 20's whole problem. Enforcing that in application code across documents is a race waiting to happen.

→ panel 20, the seat that must not be double-sold
You will probably use both

Postgres for the things that must be correct, something faster for the things that must be quick. That is not a compromise, it is the normal answer.

→ and it is two systems to operate, per panel 24
For your capstone: start relational Documents are not worse. At your data volumes nothing you choose will be slow, and the relational shape is the one that forgives a schema you got wrong. You will get it wrong. Being able to ask a question you had not thought of, without rewriting how everything is stored, is worth more in a semester project than any performance difference you will be able to measure.
