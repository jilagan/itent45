Regions, availability zones and failover

A cloud provider offers servers in regions around the world. The region you choose sets how far your servers are from your users and how well your site survives a failure.

A bank has branches in Manila and Cebu. A customer in Manila is served faster at the Manila branch. If a typhoon closes that branch, the Cebu branch can keep serving customers only if it already has a copy of the records.

Think first. If light in fiber travels 200,000 km per second, what is the fastest possible round trip between Manila and Virginia, which is 27,400 km there and back?
The answer is 27,400 divided by 200,000, which is 0.137 seconds or about 137 ms. Students who answer 69 ms have counted one direction only. The slider in the next step shows the same figure.

How the figures are worked out

The minimum round trip is the round-trip distance divided by the speed of light in glass fiber. Real networks take about 1.5 times that, because packets pass through switches and rarely travel in a straight line.

Time to first byte here is five round trips plus the 40 ms your code takes to run. No code, purchase or optimization gets below the minimum. Only moving closer does.

In "Round trips and latency", most of a page load was round trips, and more server capacity did not make a slow round trip shorter. A distant region produces the same effect.

Load balancers and data center failures

A load balancer uses health checks to stop sending traffic to a failed machine, as in "Load balancers and scaling". A data center failure takes every machine in it, so it needs one of the options in the failure step.

Two ways to copy data

Multi-AZ saves each write in both data centers before confirming it. This is called synchronous replication, and it works because the buildings are milliseconds apart.

Multi-region copies each write to Tokyo after confirming it. This is called asynchronous replication, and the newest writes can be lost if the primary region fails.

Recovery time and recovery point

The recovery time objective (RTO) is how long until you are back. The recovery point objective (RPO) is how much data you accept losing. Choose both before an outage, because afterward they are chosen for you.

Every user of your capstone is in Metro Manila, so choose Singapore. Use multi-AZ, which means two data centers in one region, if the provider offers it by default. It costs little and protects against a data center failure. Skip multi-region, which doubles the cost and needs code to resolve conflicting writes.
