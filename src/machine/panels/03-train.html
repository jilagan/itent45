03 — Where the line comes from

The line gets pushed until it stops being wrong so often

The last panel said a model is a line somebody found, and left out the finding. Here it is. Start from a line drawn at random, measure how badly it is doing, then nudge it in whatever direction makes that number smaller. Repeat a few hundred times. That is training, and there is no more to it than that.

Ask the room. If you are standing on a hillside in fog and want to reach the bottom, what can you do without seeing where the bottom is? Feel which way is downhill. Step. Repeat.

You are watching logistic regression being fitted The loss on the left is cross-entropy, the standard way of scoring a boundary that predicts a category. Steps come from the slope of that loss with respect to each of the three numbers, which is gradient descent.

Those three words appear in every textbook and every framework. This screen is all they mean.
Three numbers, and only three This line has a weight for hours, a weight for attendance, and a bias. Training adjusts those three. A language model does the identical thing to several hundred billion of them, for months, on thousands of machines. The arithmetic is the same size; the bill is not.
The step size is a choice somebody has to make Too small and it crawls, and you pay for compute that is barely moving. Too large and it leaps past the bottom and comes down the other side, over and over, and the loss climbs instead of falling. Nothing detects this for you. It is the most common reason a training run produces nothing and the most common thing a beginner fiddles with.

Try the largest setting and watch the loss curve go the wrong way.
