Recurrent networks: the approach before transformers

Imagine sitting through a long lecture with a single sticky note. After each sentence you rewrite the note to summarize everything so far, and then you forget the sentence. At the end you can answer questions only from the note.

Until about 2017, language models read text this way. They are called recurrent neural networks. The model reads left to right. After each word it updates one small note, called the hidden state, that summarizes everything read so far, and then it moves on without the word. The note is a fixed size, so each new word pushes something older out.

This panel shows the problems that causes. The fix is covered later in "Attention and transformers".

Think first. In the sentence The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired, what does it refer to? Now imagine you had to answer using only a short summary that you rewrote after each word. Which words would you expect that summary to keep most clearly?

What is left on the note

Each word that was read, with the share of it that is still on the note.

Two candidates for it

it stands for a thing, so the candidates are the two nouns, animal and street. No other word in the sentence can be what it refers to.

The note is one fixed size
The note stays the same size however long the sentence is. A short sentence fits comfortably. A paragraph does not, and nothing tells you which parts were squeezed out.

A network that works this way is called recurrent. The same small step is applied over and over. Each time, it takes the previous note and the next word and produces a new note.
Two problems Distance. Early words fade, so anything that depends on a connection across a long gap is lost.

Order. Word fifty cannot be read until word forty-nine has been read. Training could not be spread across many machines, which limited how much text anyone could train on.
One fix for both problems Stop summarizing. Keep every word, and let each word look directly at all the others, weighing them by how well they fit. Every word is then one step from every other word, and all the comparisons can happen at once.
