13 — What you are about to watch

Give it tools and it can be wrong and find out

On its own, a model reasons in a closed room. Give it a shell, a file system and a test runner and something changes: it can take an action and get an answer back from the world. That is the difference between a chat and an agent, and it is one line type.

Ask the room. Which of these three lines could not have been written by the model?

Thought and Action come from the model. Observation does not. It comes from a test runner, a file system, an API, and nothing else in the loop can contradict the model.

This pattern has a name: ReAct, for reason and act.
Watch for the moment it keeps going The task is finished at step 15. Look at what it does next, then ask whether you would have caught that in review.
Four ways a loop drifts, and every one has a cause you have already seen It does not know when to stop. Stopping is a judgement. It stops when it predicts a stopping token, which is sometimes too early and sometimes never.

The window fills. Every thought, action and observation gets appended. Twenty steps in, your original instruction may not be in front of it any more. Panel 05, arriving with a bill.

Errors compound. A wrong observation at step three gets treated as fact for every step after it. Confidence does not decay on its own.

It gets dearer as it goes. You pay per step, the context grows each time, and attention cost rises with the square of the context. The twentieth step costs more than the first for the same work.
