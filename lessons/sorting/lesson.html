Sorting and searching

Putting a list in order, called sorting, takes work once. After that, any item can be found in a few guesses.

On enlistment day, 40 student cards pile up on the registrar's desk. To find one ID, a clerk flips through the pile card by card. If the cards are in ID order, the clerk opens the pile in the middle, sees which half holds the ID and repeats on that half.

Think first. You must find one ID in 1,000 unsorted cards and in 1,000 sorted cards. About how many cards do you check in each pile?
Most students say 1,000 for both, or 500 for the unsorted pile on average. Collect guesses before step 3. The sorted answer is 10.

These cards are face down. When you compare two cards, you learn which one is larger. That check is a comparison. Put the cards in order, smallest on the left, with as few comparisons as you can.

How few comparisons are enough?

Eight cards can be arranged in 40,320 ways. Each comparison can at best cut the ways in half. Fifteen halvings leave 32,768 ways, which is too few, so 16 comparisons are the least that always works. Comparing every pair of cards takes 28.

In a sorted list, one guess can rule out half of the cells. Finding an item this way is called binary search. IDs rise from left to right.

Each guess halves the cells still open. A list of 15 needs at most 4 guesses, 31 needs 5 and 63 needs 6. Doubling the list adds one guess.

Sorting costs work once, so it pays off for a list that is searched many times.

You can now sort a list by comparing, find an item by halving, and say how many guesses a list needs.

Guesses for larger lists

A list of 1,000 needs at most 10 guesses. One million needs 20, and one billion needs 30.

When sorting is not worth it

Sorting 500 cards takes thousands of comparisons. A list searched only once is faster to scan from the start.

In databases

An index is a sorted copy of one column, so the database can halve its search. Without an index, a search reads every row, as in The N+1 query problem. SQL basics shows how queries ask for rows.
