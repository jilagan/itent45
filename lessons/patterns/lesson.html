Spotting patterns

A pattern is something that repeats in data. Finding patterns lets you describe many records at once and spot the one that does not fit.

A sari-sari store owner reads a week of receipts. On payday Friday, cooking oil and soy sauce sell out. Before a typhoon, candles and canned goods sell out. Noticing what repeats lets her stock the shelf before customers ask. Finding what repeats in data is called pattern recognition.

Think first. Here are five days of milk tea sales: 20, 22, 21, 60, 20 cups. Which day is unusual, and what might explain it?
Most students pick the fourth day. Ask for two or three possible reasons, such as a holiday, a school event or a counting error, and ask what they would check first.

Counting pairs

Stores call this market basket analysis. It counts how often items share a receipt. A pair on half the receipts is a strong pattern. A pair on two receipts may be chance.

How the length is counted

The shortened message is the block plus one symbol for the repeat count. A block of 4 letters repeated 3 times costs 4 + 1 = 5 symbols.

Compression in practice

Zip files and image formats find repeated pieces and store each piece once. Data with many repeats shrinks a lot. Data with few repeats shrinks little. Storing a repeated fact once is also the idea in Normalization: storing each fact once.
