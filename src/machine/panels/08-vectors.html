08 — How words get compared

Meaning turns into a position, and positions can be subtracted

Tokens are numbers, and numbers do not have meanings. So each one becomes a vector: a position in a space with a few hundred directions. Words that turn up in similar company end up in similar places. There is no labelling step anywhere in it.

Ask the room. If enrol, register and sign up land near each other without anyone declaring them synonyms, what else might be sitting near your search terms?

One vector per word is the old version Here bank would get a single point, somewhere between the river and the money. Modern models give a word a different vector depending on the sentence around it, Retrieval works as well as it now does because of that change.
What to take from the contested example Two things are true at once and students will want to collapse them. Embeddings do absorb the statistics of the text they were fitted to, and that has consequences when the space sits inside a hiring screen or a scholarship filter. And the famous analogies overstate how cleanly you can read that off, because the method is arranged.

The version worth carrying: a model reflects its corpus, you cannot prompt that away because it is not an opinion, and anyone claiming a tidy number for how biased a model is should be asked how they measured it.
The same trick, applied to whole sentences

A sentence gets a position too, worked out from the words in it. Once two sentences are positions, asking how alike they are becomes a question about the angle between them. Pick any two and compare the score with the number of words they actually share.

The number is a cosine: 1 when two arrows point the same way, 0 when they are at right angles. It measures direction only, so a long sentence and a short one can still score highly if they are about the same thing.
This is what makes search work on meaning rather than spelling A student types how do I sign up for a class. Your help page is titled course enrolment process. They share almost nothing as text, and a keyword search returns nothing useful. Compared as positions they are nearly the same direction.

Turn it around and the point is sharper. How do I drop a class shares five words with the sign-up question and means the opposite, and the score says so. Word overlap and meaning are different measurements, and only one of them is the one you wanted.
Why this matters an hour from now If similar meanings sit near each other, then find me the nearest chunks to this question becomes a meaningful thing to compute. That is what retrieval does when you point an agent at your documents. A student asking how to stop a double enrolment can be matched to a page about idempotency keys without the two sharing a single word.
