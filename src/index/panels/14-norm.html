The same fact, written down in forty places

An agent will happily design your schema, and the first one it offers usually works. Whether it keeps working depends on a question no test will ask: when one fact changes, how many rows have to change with it? Here are the same enrolments stored two ways.

Ask the room. A course changes its title. In a spreadsheet, how many cells do you edit? Now: how sure are you that you got all of them?

The flat table repeats the title once per enrolment. The split version stores it once and points at it.

Neither is wrong. The flat one answers "show me this student's schedule" without a join. People keep proposing it for that reason. It just cannot change its mind cheaply.
The rule under all of this Store each fact once, and point at it from everywhere else that needs it. A title living in one row can be wrong, but it cannot be inconsistent. A title living in four hundred rows can disagree with itself, and nothing in your code will notice, because every one of those rows is individually valid. This shape prevents that failure. An agent will not warn you about it, because the schema it wrote does work.
