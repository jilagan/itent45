Regions, availability zones and failover

Think of a bank with branches in Manila and Cebu. A customer in Manila is served faster at the Manila branch. If a typhoon closes that branch, the Cebu branch can keep serving customers only if it already has a copy of the records.

Cloud providers organize their locations in a similar way. A region is a geographic area that contains several data centers, like a city. An availability zone is one data center in a region, like one building in that city. Zones in the same region are far enough apart to fail separately and close enough to copy data between them almost instantly. Failover means switching to a standby copy when the main one fails.

Latency is the delay before a response arrives, and it depends on distance. In "Round trips and latency", most of a page load was round trips, and more server capacity did not make a slow round trip shorter. Choosing a region trades speed, survival and cost against each other.

Think first. Light in fiber travels about 200,000 km per second. A round trip between Manila and Virginia covers 27,400 km. What is the fastest a response could come back on a perfect network with no server at all?

The minimum round trip is the round-trip distance divided by the speed of light in glass fiber. Real networks take about 1.5 times that, because packets pass through switches and rarely travel in a straight line.

Time to first byte is the wait before the first part of the page arrives. Here it is five round trips plus the 40 ms your code takes to run.

No code, purchase or optimization gets below this minimum. Only moving closer does.

What this means for your capstone Every user of your capstone is in Metro Manila, so choose Singapore. Use multi-AZ (two data centers in one region) if the provider offers it by default, because it costs little and protects against a data center failure. Do not use multi-region. It doubles the cost, needs code to resolve conflicting writes, and accepts a window of lost data, all to survive an event that has never happened to your users. Multi-region makes sense when a region outage costs more than running two of everything. Know that number before you decide.
