06 — What it actually reads

It has never seen a letter in its life

Text does not reach the model as text. It is cut into tokens first, and the model only ever sees the numbers those tokens map to. Everything below the cut is invisible: the letters, the spelling, the fact that two words rhyme.

What this explains
Why it miscounts letters. Asking it how many r's are in a word it received as three chunks is like asking you to count brushstrokes in printed text. The information was gone before it arrived.

Why there is a size limit. The window is measured in tokens, never in words or pages.

Why the bill looks like that. You are charged per token, so the same sentence costs different amounts in different languages.
Not every language costs the same English averages about four characters a token because most of the training text was English. Filipino and Tagalog fragment harder, so the same meaning takes more tokens, costs more to send, and fills the window faster. It fell out of what the tokenizer was fitted to, and the people paying for it were not in the room.
