Writing steps for a robot

A computer follows exact steps and cannot guess. A robot that makes milk tea shows what exact means and how a repeat handles a queue of any length.

    What the robot checks

    The robot stops at the first step it cannot do. It checks four rules.

    • Take cup comes first, and the robot holds one cup at a time.
    • Nothing can be added after Seal.
    • Serve needs a sealed cup.
    • The cup must hold exactly the items in the order, with each item added once.

    Why exact steps matter

    A computer does what the instruction says, including when the writer meant something else. The three common mistakes are a missing step, steps in the wrong order, and steps that never end.

      Three kinds of step

      Most programs combine three things: steps in order, a repeat, and a choice between steps based on a condition. Decision rules and tables covers conditions.

      A repeat that never ends

      A rule such as "Until the queue is empty" stops only if each pass removes an order. A group of steps that leaves the queue unchanged runs forever.

      Any reader that follows instructions needs exact ones. "Make the form better" leaves an AI assistant to guess. "Keep only name, email and phone" gives it exact steps and a way to check the result.
