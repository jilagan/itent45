From asset-heavy to asset-light

The technical argument for cloud is elasticity. The argument that actually gets it approved is an accounting one. Owning servers is capital expenditure. A large sum spent up front, capitalized and depreciated over years, committed before you know what you need. Renting them is operational expenditure. A monthly bill for what you actually used, expensed as you go.

Ask the room the enlistment system needed seven servers for the twenty minutes of enlistment morning. What were those seven servers doing on the other 364 days?
Asset-heavy · CapEx

You own the infrastructure

Servers, data centre, enterprise licences. A large sum committed up front and depreciated over several years.

Predictable but inflexible. You must guess your peak years ahead, and you are wrong in one of two expensive directions.
Asset-light · OpEx

You rent it by the hour

Compute time, storage, SaaS subscriptions. Expensed monthly, attributable to the thing that caused it.

Lower entry cost and elastic, but the meter never stops. A forgotten test environment bills you every month until someone notices.

Total cost of ownership: the parts of the bill nobody quotes you

The purchase price is the part everyone compares. It is rarely the part that decides.

Acquisition

Purchase price, taxes, shipping, installation. The only number that appears in most comparisons.

Operating

Power, cooling, bandwidth, licences, consumables. Runs every hour whether anyone uses the system or not.

Maintenance & support

Servicing, repairs, vendor support contracts, the patching someone has to do on a Saturday.

Training

Getting staff able to operate it. Reappears every time a person leaves.

Downtime

What it costs while it is unavailable, planned or not. On enlistment morning this is the largest number on this page.

Disposal

Decommissioning, data destruction, recycling. Real, and never in the original business case.

Discussion, take five minutes on this How might a startup benefit differently from OpEx than a large enterprise? Push them past "it is cheaper", because for a large enterprise at steady load it often is not. Useful angles: who can afford to be wrong about their guess, who already owns a data centre they have not finished depreciating, and who needs to survive long enough to have a capacity problem at all.
