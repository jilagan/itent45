12 — Saying what you actually want

A better prompt removes the answers you did not want

Students arrive believing in magic words. There are none. Panel 01 showed the model choosing from a spread of plausible continuations, and a vague request leaves that spread wide. Every constraint you add rules some of it out, and what is left is more likely to be the thing you meant.

Ask the room. Ask for "a function to enrol a student". How many different working answers could that describe? Then look at which one it is most likely to hand you.

The uncomfortable part With nothing specified, the single most likely answer is the one that reads the seat count, checks it, and then creates the row. It is careful-looking, it is what most tutorials show, and it is wrong the moment two people click at once. You have already seen that failure twice: in the transactions panel of the Module 1 tool, and in the review drill at the end of this one.

The model picks it because it is the most common shape in everything ever written about enrolling a student, and nothing you said ruled it out.
